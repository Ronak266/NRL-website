# Proposed complete CORE pickup robot

The photographed four-wheel chassis is the starting point. Its missing pickup hardware is completed here as a design proposal. Relative model units are used; this is not measured manufacturing CAD.

The front module contains a geared servo driving a bored pinion between two opposing racks. Each rack moves one carriage along two transverse guide shafts. Machined fingers and rubber pads close symmetrically on the outside of the yellow CORE. Pinion travel and jaw travel share the same ratio in the animation.

A second, chassis-mounted servo drives a vertical rack attached to the moving carriage. Two vertical shafts and bored linear bearings constrain the lift. The platform carries the gripper servo, racks, jaws and their supports together. The CORE follows the carriage only when the jaws are closed. Fixed pickup rests support the CORE after lowering and release. Mounting windows, crossbars, feet, gears, output shafts, brackets, bearings and fasteners appear as separate physical parts in disassembly.

The visible power layout includes a battery, main switch, fuse holder, controller, servo regulator/distribution block and distinct leads for both servos and all four drive motors. Servo cables flex with carriage travel in the assembled interactive view. Routes illustrate connection intent rather than a pin-level wiring diagram or verified electrical circuit.

Use **Test Pickup** to inspect the ordered open, grip, lift, lower and release cycle. The manual controls close the jaws before lifting and prevent opening an elevated gripper. Disassembly returns the moving mechanism to neutral before revealing its hardware. Soldered board components remain attached.

Before physical fabrication, choose the actual servos, battery and regulator, measure the kit mounting interfaces and CORE, and validate loads, travel, clearances, servo torque and electrical ratings. The animation demonstrates the proposed linkage; it does not establish those engineering results or competition compliance.
